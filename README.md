# Bioinformatics pipeline
This repository is a compilation of codes (Bash and R) for our supervised project. The team is composed by Ayoub KARROUCHI, Corentin LAOT and Manon SECLET, students in M1 BGE.

## Presentation of the project
The initial research contributes to the **PADMé project** through the **ARMeRIE program**, which is part of a larger initiative launched in January 2021 by **Muriel VIDAL**, a teacher-researcher at the University of West Brittany in Brest (UBO). She oversees research activities within the “Archives Sédimentaires, Transferts, paléo-Environnements” team.  
This project is being conducted within the **CReAAH UMR 6566** unit with our supervisor **Frédérique HUBLER** in Rennes, in collaboration with the Geo-Ocean research unit (UMR 6538) at the University Institute for Marine Science Research (IUEM) in Brittany.  
The period between the end of the Mesolithic and the beginning of the Neolithic was a pivotal moment in human history, marked by the progressive transition from hunter-gatherers to farmers (González-Fortes et al., 2017). This transition induced direct and indirect major changes in soil bacterial communities, plant composition and land use practices. These changes must be studied to understand how the current biological composition of coastal sediments evolved over time, and what traces early agricultural practices may have left in the sedimentary record.  
The site’s history of successive flooding and drainage events is a particularly relevant factor in this evolutionary process : these events have likely altered the balance between aerobic and anaerobic bacterial groups, and between plant assemblages characteristic of marine and continental environments, leaving distinct biological signatures in the sedimentary archives.  
Against this backdrop, our tutor-supervised project seeks to identify plant and bacterial taxa through metagenomic analysis of sediment core from the Kerallé Valley, with a first focus on characterising the broader rhizosphere microbiome and its temporal dynamics. We aim to study major chronological periods : the **Mesolithic**, the **Neolithic**, the **Bronze Age**, the **Iron Age**, the **Roman Empire**, the **Middle Ages** and the **post-Middle Ages**.
